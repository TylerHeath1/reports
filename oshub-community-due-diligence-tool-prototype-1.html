What's different here

Today, paralegals across Pakistan, India, Sri Lanka, and Vietnam already collect worker complaints — but each tool has its own format, language coverage is patchy, and the brand-chain connection is manual and slow.

Loose input. The paralegal types what she hears, in Urdu, with no required form fields. Voice notes are captured. The system, not the paralegal, does the matching.
Native language. Urdu first, English second. Tamil, Sinhala, Bangla, Vietnamese, Mandarin available depending on country. The data layer is multilingual, not just the UI.
Mobile-first. Most field paralegals don't own laptops. The capture experience assumes a phone, intermittent connectivity, one hand free.
It's not a grievance mechanism. Workers don't file complaints here directly. This is the data layer that powers the existing CBM partners — MyVox in Pakistan, Fair Wear's helplines, union case files — wherever community-based monitoring already happens.
