What if a garment worker had the same supply-chain intelligence as the CEO of the brand she sews for?

Open Supply Hub is building the open data + connection layer that puts community-based due diligence into the hands of the people actually doing the work — advocates, paralegals, unions, NGOs. This prototype walks through how that might look.

📌This is an example, not a finished product. It illustrates a direction. Real data, real partners, real cases — rendered as a clickable concept to react to.
▌ The situation

Aisha Khan · paralegal lead at PILER, Karachi

Aisha runs community-based due diligence (CBDD) for a region of Pakistan's garment sector. She and her team of 17 paralegals work with women's cafés, unions, and grassroots groups across Karachi, Lahore, and Faisalabad to surface labour-rights issues at the factories where workers are most exposed.

This week, 340 workers at Apex Garments Mills reported three months of unpaid overtime and retaliation threats. Sumera, 26, told a paralegal at the Faisalabad Legal Aid Centre. Forty-six of her colleagues corroborated within days.

For Aisha's team, the question isn't "is this happening?" — they know. The question is: which brand sources from this factory, who has influence over them, what mechanisms apply, and which path the affected workers want to push. Today, answering those questions takes weeks of manual research. This prototype shows what it looks like when OS Hub is the open data + connection layer underneath their work.

Problem

Worker voice is loud — but unconnected to the actors with influence.

5,082 cases logged in Pakistan since 2022 — most never reach the brands sourcing from those factories.

Solution

Open data + connection layer beneath community-based DD.

Combine rights-holder data with corporate chains, legislation, brand policies, and accountability pathways — in the worker's language.

Outcome

From a month of research to hours.

Cases reach an accountability mechanism fast enough to recover wages and protect from retaliation — money and safety.

Impact

5M workers reached at scale, 25M production locations mapped.

Communities + advocates + brands operate on the same data — patterns become systemic change.
